01

Practitioner Framing

Coverage bankers live on two things: being first to know something is happening, and showing up with a credible hook. Every signal below maps to one of those two needs. Deterministic and quantitative signals are the best kind because they are defensible in a Monday morning call — "Three 13D filers crossed 5% in industrials names this week" is a conversation opener. Qualitative or sentiment signals are secondary and covered separately.

The framework below organizes signals into four deal categories. Each signal maps to a specific SEC form type, a specific edgartools API call, a processing tier (same-day, next morning, or weekly), and a concrete banker action.

Core library: All SEC data access in this framework uses edgartools (pip install edgartools). It handles EDGAR HTTP communication, rate limiting, XML/HTML parsing, and XBRL extraction. Custom parsing code is written only where edgartools has no dedicated data object (primarily Schedule 13D/G text extraction and DEF 14A proxy data).

Tier Definitions

Tier Timing Action Examples
TIER 1 Same day Immediate banker call / board alert 13D activist filing, 8-K bankruptcy, CEO sudden exit
TIER 2 Next morning Coverage team briefing note Leverage ratio breach, Form D >$50M, restatement
TIER 3 Weekly digest Pipeline update / screening output Valuation discount screen, pack formation, capex surge

M&A Target Screening M&A

Identify companies statistically likely to be acquired or to acquire — before a deal is announced. The goal is to arrive at the CFO's office with a prepared pitch before any RFP is issued.

13D / 13G — Activist stake accumulation
Tier 1

When a Schedule 13D is filed, an entity has crossed 5% and declared intent to influence management. An activist campaign almost always ends in one of three outcomes: sale of the company, a spin-off/breakup, or a defensive M&A transaction. All three are IB mandates. Call the CFO the same day.

A 13G → 13D amendment is an even stronger signal: a previously passive holder has decided to get active.

Forms SC 13D SC 13D/A SC 13G/A
13F pack formation — Institutional herding
Tier 3

When 3+ top-tier funds all accumulate meaningful new positions in the same company within the same quarter, they signal institutional conviction that something is going to happen — either deep undervaluation awaiting a catalyst, or information advantage. A banker should be pitching that company a strategic review.

Compare ThirteenF.infotable against previous_holding_report() across all filers to identify tickers appearing as new positions in 3+ funds simultaneously.

Forms 13F-HR
XBRL: EV / EBITDA — Valuation discount
Tier 3

Any company trading at more than 1.5 standard deviations below its sector (SIC code) median EV/EBITDA is a logical acquisition target — either a PE buyout or a strategic acquirer paying a control premium. Compute this systematically from XBRL facts across the coverage universe.

Pull OperatingIncomeLoss, LongTermDebt, and Assets from company.get_facts().to_pandas(). Requires market cap augmentation from a price data source (e.g. yfinance).

Forms 10-K 10-Q (XBRL)
8-K Item 5.02 — Sudden C-suite departure
Tier 1

A same-day or immediately effective resignation of a CEO or CFO — especially with vague language ("pursuing other opportunities") and no named successor — is a near-certain signal of corporate distress or board conflict. The company either needs a strategic review banker immediately or an activist already has one coming.

Flag: effective date = today + no successor named + C-suite role. Use eightk.has_item("5.02") and parse the item text.

Forms 8-K Item 5.02
8-K Item 2.01 — Asset disposition signal
Tier 2

A completion of acquisition or disposition of assets signals a company actively reshaping its portfolio. A series of divestitures across multiple 8-K 2.01 filings within 12 months is a strong indicator of a broader strategic process — either breakup, carve-out, or preparation for sale. Flag companies with 2+ Item 2.01 filings in a rolling 12-month window.

Forms 8-K Item 2.01

from collections import defaultdict

new_positions = defaultdict(list)  # ticker → [fund names]

Capital Markets Origination ECM / DCM

Identify companies that need to raise capital — equity, debt, or convertible — before they issue an RFP. The best mandate conversations happen six months before the company knows it needs a banker.

XBRL: Leverage creep approaching refi wall
Tier 2

Net Debt / EBITDA drifting upward over 4–6 consecutive quarters and approaching 4–5×, combined with a debt maturity within 18–36 months. This company will need to refinance — new bond, leveraged loan refi, or equity raise to delever. Entirely derivable from XBRL.

Track: LongTermDebt, ShortTermBorrowings, OperatingIncomeLoss, DepreciationAndAmortization across trailing 6 quarters. Flag when ratio increased 3+ consecutive periods.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)
XBRL: FCF acceleration + underleveraged
Tier 3

Strong and accelerating free cash flow with almost no debt. These companies either become acquisition currency or get pitched a leveraged recapitalization to return capital. Threshold: FCF/Revenue >15%, Net Debt/EBITDA <1×, and FCF growth >20% YoY for two consecutive years.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)
Form D — Large private placement closed
Tier 2

A Form D filing above $50M means a private company just raised a significant equity round and is on a trajectory toward IPO or sale within 2–5 years. For ECM, this is a pipeline seeding call — not an immediate deal, but a relationship to initiate now. edgartools provides a FormD data object via filing.obj().

Forms Form D
XBRL: Capex surge vs. revenue base
Tier 2

A company whose capital expenditure jumps to >15% of revenue — a step-change from historical 3–5% — is building something large: data centers, manufacturing, infrastructure. That capex often cannot be funded from operating cash flow alone, creating a near-certain financing need within 12–24 months.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)

04

Restructuring Advisory RX

Find companies in financial distress before they file Chapter 11 — because the restructuring advisory mandate goes to whoever is already in the room. Early entry is everything.

8-K Item 1.03 — Bankruptcy / receivership
Tier 1

A bankruptcy or receivership filing is the most severe distress signal — at this point the restructuring advisory is already in play, but there may be time for an advisor not yet engaged. Alert immediately and check for existing RX coverage.

Forms 8-K Item 1.03
8-K Item 4.02 — Financial restatement
Tier 1

Non-reliance on previously issued financial statements signals accounting problems. Downstream consequences — SEC inquiry, auditor change, possible fraud investigation — often push a company into a distressed sale or bankruptcy. Restatements frequently precede a strategic alternatives process by 6–18 months.

Forms 8-K Item 4.02
XBRL: Interest coverage < 1.5×
Tier 2

EBIT / Interest Expense below 1.5× means the company is not comfortably covering its debt service from operations. Below 1.0× means it is technically not covering at all. The most direct financial distress indicator computable from XBRL. Compound with leverage ratio for a distress score.

XBRL fields: OperatingIncomeLoss ÷ InterestExpense. Flag any company where this ratio has declined for 3 consecutive quarters and is now below 1.5×.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)
XBRL: Current ratio deterioration
Tier 2

Current Assets / Current Liabilities trending below 1.0× over 2–3 consecutive quarters combined with negative operating cash flow is liquidity distress. The company may look solvent on paper but is running out of cash for near-term obligations — the "wall of maturities" + "shrinking liquidity runway" scenario.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)
8-K Item 2.06 — Material impairment (repeated)
Tier 2

An asset write-down is management admitting they overpaid for something or that a business unit has deteriorated below book value. When impairments cluster — multiple 8-K 2.06 filings across consecutive quarters — it signals fundamental business deterioration that may require a balance sheet restructuring. Quantify the impairment amount from the 8-K text relative to total assets from XBRL to assess materiality. Flag companies with 2+ Item 2.06 filings in 18 months.

Forms 8-K Item 2.06 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL)
05

Activism Defense Advisory Defense

Get to a company before the activist arrives — or the moment the activist arrives — to pitch defensive advisory, a friendly strategic review, or a pre-emptive value creation plan. Being second means no mandate.

13D by known activist — Primary trigger
Tier 1

When a known activist (Elliott, Starboard, Icahn, Pershing, Trian, Third Point, ValueAct) files a 13D, the target company needs a financial advisor to prepare a response. The advisor who shows up first with a strategic alternatives analysis wins the mandate. Cross-reference every SC 13D filer CIK against the activist watchlist (Section 08).

Forms SC 13D
13F ownership churn — Weak shareholder base
Tier 3

High churn in institutional ownership — many institutions exiting, being replaced by shorter-duration holders — signals weakening sponsorship. Activist funds specifically look for this because it means the shareholder base will not resist them. Measure as: institutions present in Q-4 but gone by Q0, divided by total Q0 holders.

Compute the Herfindahl index of ownership concentration across 4 rolling quarters. A declining index alongside high turnover is the soft-target signature.

Forms 13F-HR
XBRL: TSR gap vs. sector peers
Tier 3

The empirical activist playbook: find a company with 3-year TSR significantly below its sector median and an identifiable operational or capital allocation fix. Screening for this before the activist does allows a banker to walk in and say: "here is what the activists will argue, and here is what you should do about it." XBRL provides the operational metrics; price data needed for TSR.

Forms 10-K / 10-Q (XBRL) Market data (yfinance)
DEF 14A — Director tenure / pay gap
Tier 3

Activists routinely attack boards where directors have served 15+ years (entrenchment) and where executive compensation is decoupled from TSR. Systematic scanning of DEF 14A filings for average director tenure and CEO pay-vs-TSR correlation gives you the vulnerability map before any public campaign. Parse via filing.text() as edgartools has no dedicated proxy data object yet.

Forms DEF 14A (Proxy)

Daily Processing Architecture Infrastructure

All signals are computed in a nightly batch. The pipeline is split by urgency: Tier 1 event-driven signals run first and fast; XBRL financial screeners run later as they require iterating across the full coverage universe.

6:00 PM
8-K event sweep — Tier 1 signals
get_all_current_filings(form="8-K") → route each filing by item code. Items 1.03, 4.02 → restructuring desk. Item 5.02 sudden departure → M&A / activism. Item 2.06 → RX monitoring. All Tier 1 hits generate immediate banker alerts.
7:00 PM
13D / 13G activist sweep — Tier 1
get_all_current_filings(form="SC 13D") and SC 13G. Cross-reference filer CIK against activist watchlist. Known activist → Tier 1 same-day alert. Unknown filer with 3+ historical 13Ds → watchlist candidate flagged for review.
8:00 PM
Form D sweep — ECM pipeline seeding
get_all_current_filings(form="D") → parse FormD object. Filter: total offering amount >$50M, equity security type. Tier 2 alert: ECM coverage initiation recommendation.
9:00 PM
XBRL financial screener refresh — Tier 2 / 3
For each company in the coverage universe (~500–2,000 tickers): company.get_facts().to_pandas() → compute leverage, interest coverage, current ratio, FCF, capex ratio. Update silver tables → gold screener views. Outputs feed weekly digest.
Quarterly
13F season — Pack formation + ownership churn (Tier 3)
Run ~45 days after each quarter end. Sweep get_filings(form="13F-HR") across all tracked funds. Compute pack formation (3+ new positions same ticker), ownership churn (Herfindahl decline), and QoQ position deltas. Outputs feed the weekly strategic digest.
07

Signal Scoring & Deal Routing

Individual signals are interesting. A company hitting three independent signals in the same rolling 30-day window is a mandate conversation. The scoring system below produces a composite score per company; any company reaching a threshold triggers a banker brief routed to the appropriate deal desk.

13D by known activist (Tier 1 auto-alert) +5
Sudden CEO / CFO departure (8-K 5.02) +4
8-K bankruptcy / restatement (1.03 / 4.02) +5
Pack formation (3+ new 13F holders) +3
EV/EBITDA >1.5σ below sector median +3
Interest coverage < 1.5× (declining) +3
Leverage ratio trending up 3+ quarters +2
High institutional ownership churn +2
Form D >$50M equity raise +2
Capex / Revenue surged >15% +2
Alert threshold: Any company scoring ≥5 points in a rolling 30-day window generates a banker brief. The brief includes: company name, signals triggered, relevant deal type, key XBRL metrics, and the suggested outreach hook.

08

Activist Watchlist — CIK Seed List

No canonical open-source activist watchlist exists as a maintained, downloadable dataset. The closest publicly available resource is the dokson/hedge-fund-tracker GitHub repository, which maintains a community-curated CSV of fund CIKs — but it covers performance-driven institutional funds broadly, not activists specifically, and has no maintenance guarantee.

The right approach is to bootstrap the watchlist from EDGAR's own 13D filing history. Any entity that has filed 3+ distinct SC 13D campaigns against different targets in the past 5 years is definitionally an activist. This is fully derivable using edgartools and self-updates as part of the nightly batch.

Multi-entity problem: Elliott, Icahn, and others operate through many legal entities, each with a distinct CIK. Elliott alone has filed under Elliott Associates L.P., Elliott International L.P., and Elliott International Capital Advisors Inc. Any watchlist must group aliases under a canonical fund name — verify via EDGAR company lookup and match by address and signatories.

Data Gaps & Augmentation

edgartools covers all SEC filing retrieval and parsing. Two areas require external augmentation for the quantitative signal framework to be fully self-contained.

Gap Why Needed Recommended Source Cost
Stock price / market cap Required for EV calculations (EV/EBITDA screener) and TSR comparison for activism defense signals. XBRL does not include market data. yfinance (Python) for daily close; any financial data API for intraday Free (yfinance)
Activist fund CIK aliases Multi-entity filing structure means a single activist fund may use 3–5 different CIKs. Static seed list requires manual verification and ongoing maintenance. Bootstrap from EDGAR 13D history (see Section 08) + quarterly manual review Free (EDGAR)
13D beneficial ownership % edgartools has no structured Schedule13D data object. The beneficial ownership percentage must be parsed from filing.text(). Regex is reliable for the standard cover page format (~80% of filings) but fails on non-standard formats. Regex on filing.text() for now; evaluate Apify SEC 13D scraper or EDGAR full-text search API for edge cases Free (regex); paid (Apify)
DEF 14A structured proxy data Director tenure and compensation data for activism vulnerability scoring requires parsing proxy statement tables. edgartools has no dedicated proxy data object — text-only access via filing.text(). Parse via filing.text() + structured extraction; or ISS/Glass Lewis data feed (enterprise cost) Free (parsing); $$$$ (ISS/Glass Lewis)
Debt maturity schedule Required for the refi-wall signal. Maturity dates are disclosed in 10-K footnotes and occasionally 8-K Item 1.01 — not in structured XBRL fields. Parse from tenk.get_section_text("Long-Term Debt") footnotes; or Bloomberg / Capital IQ for enterprise use Free (parsing); $$$$ (Bloomberg)
Summary: For a fully free implementation, the only two external dependencies are yfinance for price data and a manually maintained activist CIK CSV. Every other signal in this framework is derivable entirely from edgartools + EDGAR. The DEF 14A and debt maturity signals require custom text parsing but no paid data sources.
